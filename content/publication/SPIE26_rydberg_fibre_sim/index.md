abstract: "Rydberg atom-based electrometry is intrinsically traceable to the International System of Units (SI) through atomic constants. However, the atoms measure the electric field inside a dielectric vapour cell held within a physical sensor assembly, whereas calibration must relate to the incident field. To quantify this relationship, we simulate a complete fibre-coupled sensor comprising a vapour cell, mechanical cradle, and collimation optics from 1 GHz to 18 GHz using a full-wave electromagnetic solver. Six geometric, material and alignment parameters are swept to obtain sensitivity coefficients, which are combined into a ‘Guide to the expression of uncertainty in measurement’ (GUM) uncertainty budget for the field scaling factor. The dominant perturbation arises from the cradle and optical components rather than from the vapour cell itself. The combined standard uncertainty in the scaling factor is 3.3 %, 5.7 % and 2.7 % at the three persistent spectral features of the assembly (6.0, 8.9 and 16.0 GHz, respectively) and reaches a maximum of 13 % near 4.8 GHz. By comparison, the uncertainty remains below about 3.0 % across the entire band for the bare cubic cell. For the cradle sensor design, vertical positioning of the optical beam within the cell and RF azimuthal alignment are the largest contributors to the uncertainty budget, while uncertainty in the glass permittivity contributes less than 0.5 % throughout. This decomposition provides quantitative guidance for the design of the next-generation sensor cradle."
